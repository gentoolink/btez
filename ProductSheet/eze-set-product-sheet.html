The system

Three parts. Zero fuss.

01 Screw anchor

One piece, threaded to the point. Drives straight into soft or uneven ground and stays put. No excavation, no poured base.

02 Adjuster plate

Threads up and down the shaft to set height. Your grade control lives on the anchor itself, so fine-tuning never means re-digging.

03 Pipe cradle — the saddle head

The U-shaped head locks onto the bolt head and grips the line. No sag, no shift, no drift. It also doubles as a hand grip for driving the anchor. Interchangeable cradles fit multiple pipe sizes.

How it works

Three steps. One tool.

01

Drive

Screw it in with a 1½″ socket, an Allen wrench, or by hand on the saddle head. The threaded auger anchors deep in seconds, even where other supports can’t bite.

02

Set

Drop the pipe into the cradle. It locks onto the bolt head and grips the line. No sag, no shift, no drift.

03

Dial in

Thread the adjuster plate up or down to hit exact grade. Fine-tune anytime right up to backfill.

On the job

Works the way you do.

Pressure systems

Complete the full piping install, then drill the holes for the “squirt” test and run it. Once testing is done, set the chamber over top. No separate chamber supports, and no zip-tying the pipes to the chamber.

Gravity-feed systems

Set the grade and run your testing. Then set the chambers over top for the final install. The line stays exactly where you dialed it in.

04 Ground pressure & surface loading

The most common cause of EZE-SET system failure is excessive ground pressure. Pressure at the surface above an installed system must not exceed 13.8 kPa (2 psi), including snow load.

  • A single-blade lawn mower may be operated over the installed system.
  • Uncompacted snow to a depth of 65 cm (26 in) is acceptable.
  • Do not drive snowmobiles, ATVs, trucks, or heavy equipment over the piping field.
  • Do not use a snow plough, tractor, or blower to pile snow onto the surface above the piping.

Driving light equipment over snow compacts it. Compacted snow loses its air pockets and turns to solid ice, gaining weight and producing ground pressure well beyond the limit above. An installation buried under compacted snow will most likely fail.
